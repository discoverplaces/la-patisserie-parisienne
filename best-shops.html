Introduction

Paris is one of the most famous cities in the world for pâtisserie. Its pastry shops are known for elegant desserts, refined flavours and beautiful presentation.

Some shops are historic and traditional, while others are modern and artistic. Together, they show how French pastry combines culture, creativity and craftsmanship.

This page presents four famous pastry shops and chefs: Ladurée, Pierre Hermé, Stohrer and Cédric Grolet.

Ladurée

Luxury Macarons and Tea Salon

Ladurée is one of the most recognised French pâtisseries and is especially famous for its elegant macarons. Its colourful macaron boxes, refined packaging and luxury tea salon atmosphere make it a symbol of Parisian elegance.

The brand is strongly associated with high-end French pastry culture and is popular among visitors who want to experience classic Parisian sweetness and sophistication.

Visit Official Website

Pierre Hermé

Creative Flavours and Modern Macarons

Pierre Hermé is famous for creative pastry ideas and original flavour combinations. His macarons are admired for their delicate texture, refined taste and artistic presentation.

Unlike traditional pastry shops that focus mainly on classic flavours, Pierre Hermé is known for modern combinations that make French pâtisserie feel innovative and exciting.

Visit Official Website

Stohrer

Historic Parisian Pâtisserie

Stohrer is known as one of the oldest pastry shops in Paris. It is closely linked to traditional French pastry heritage and is famous for classic desserts such as baba au rhum.

The shop represents the historical side of French pâtisserie, where traditional recipes and elegant decoration continue to attract pastry lovers.

Visit Official Website

Cédric Grolet

Artistic Fruit-Shaped Desserts

Cédric Grolet is known for modern and artistic pastry creations, especially his realistic fruit-shaped desserts. These pastries are carefully designed to look like real fruits while hiding delicate creams and fillings inside.

His work shows how French pastry can become a form of edible art, combining flavour, precision and visual creativity.
