The Croissant

Discover the golden, buttery and flaky pastry that has become one of the most iconic symbols of French breakfast culture.

Overview

Golden, flaky and wonderfully buttery, the croissant is one of the most famous pastries in the world. It is recognised for its crisp outer layer, soft interior and delicate crescent shape.

Although the croissant is strongly linked to France today, its story is more surprising than many people expect. It became part of French pastry culture through a mixture of history, tradition and refined baking craftsmanship.

The Surprising Origins of the Croissant

While the croissant is now seen as a symbol of French cuisine, many historians link its origin to Austria. It is often connected to the kipferl, a crescent-shaped pastry that existed long before the modern French croissant.

One well-known story connects the crescent pastry to the Ottoman siege of Vienna in 1683. According to this legend, Viennese bakers working during the night heard enemy activity and helped warn the city.

Another popular story suggests that Marie Antoinette, who came from Austria, introduced Austrian-style pastries to the French court. Over time, French bakers refined the idea and transformed it into the flaky croissant known today.

How Croissants are Enjoyed in France

In France, croissants are usually enjoyed plain because their buttery flavour is already rich and satisfying. They are often eaten fresh from a boulangerie in the morning.

Croissants are commonly served with coffee, café crème or hot chocolate. Sitting in a café with a warm croissant is often seen as a simple but classic French breakfast experience.

By the early 20th century, croissants had become a standard item in French bakeries and an essential part of French breakfast culture.

Different Types of Croissants

Although the classic butter croissant remains the most famous, bakeries and pâtisseries also create many variations using sweet and savoury fillings.

Some croissants are filled with almond cream, chocolate, pistachio or fruit fillings. Others are made savoury with ingredients such as cheese, ham or vegetables.

These variations show how the croissant continues to evolve while remaining connected to French pastry tradition.

Classic Butter Croissant

The traditional version made with buttery dough and baked until golden, crisp and flaky.

Chocolate Croissant

Also known as pain au chocolat, this pastry contains chocolate inside soft flaky layers.

Almond Croissant

A richer variation filled with almond cream and often topped with sliced almonds and icing sugar.

Savoury Croissant

A modern version filled with ingredients such as cheese, ham or vegetables for a savoury option.

Fun Fact

The croissant’s crescent shape is often linked to stories about the Ottoman crescent symbol. Although these stories combine legend and history, they add to the cultural fascination surrounding this famous pastry.

Quick Facts
  • Famous for its buttery flaky layers
  • Strongly associated with French breakfast culture
  • Inspired by earlier Austrian crescent-shaped pastries
  • Commonly found in cafés, bakeries and pâtisseries
